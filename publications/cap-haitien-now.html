Cap-Haitien Now

Cap-Haitien Now is a research publication produced by the Now Institute that focuses the broader Haiti Now investigation on Cap-Haïtien, the historical and former capital of Haiti. Building on a two-year cross-disciplinary program at UCLA Architecture and Urban Design, the volume documents the city's contemporary urban conditions and outlines a set of design and policy strategies developed in concert with Haitian governing and community partners.

In 2013, the Mayne / Now Institute SUPRASTUDIO brought the focus of the study to Cap-Haïtien, which has experienced dramatic growth in population and commercial activity over the past two decades. These changes have fueled an uncontrolled growth in informal settlements and strained the capacity of the city's roads, utilities and ecology to meet and mitigate the burgeoning demand.

As a result, the operations and locations of the city market, the quality of residential neighborhoods and access to cultural centers have diminished. According to studies by the World Bank, the population of the city more than doubled between 1995 and 2005, and its area increased tenfold in forty years — from 1.22 km² in 1960 to 12.39 km² in 2002.

The Institute's aim is to respond to these conditions and to recognize Cap-Haïtien's existing cultural and natural assets as opportunities for urban development, economic growth and community enhancement. Rather than treating the city as a blank slate, the study reads its markets, waterfront, agricultural hinterland and historic core as the foundation for a resilient future.

Cap-Haitien Now translates this research into a coherent framework of proposals that meet specific community needs, bridging the gap between humanitarian relief and long-term planning. It positions the city as a model for how post-disaster development in Haiti can proceed through culturally grounded, locally accepted strategies.

NOW Institute Assessment

The Institute's analysis of Cap-Haïtien identified a set of interlocking challenges and opportunities that frame the city's next phase of development:

  1. Uncontrolled expansion of informal settlements, outpacing the reach of roads, utilities and public services.
  2. Displacement and congestion of the historic city market, a central engine of the local economy.
  3. Mounting environmental pressure on the bay, wetlands and the surrounding agricultural land.
  4. Diminished access to cultural centers and public space within residential neighborhoods.
  5. An underutilized agricultural hinterland capable of supporting food security and local livelihoods.

Urban Agriculture

Among the strategies advanced in Cap-Haitien Now, urban agriculture emerges as a central opportunity to couple the city's cultural and ecological assets with pressing needs for food security and employment. The surrounding plain and riverine landscape, long central to the region's economy, offer a productive framework that can be reintegrated into the growing urban fabric.

The proposal envisions a network of cultivated corridors, market gardens and productive open space that structures new development rather than being displaced by it. By embedding agriculture within the city, the plan aims to stabilize the edges of informal settlements, manage flooding and stormwater, and supply fresh produce to local markets.

Beyond its economic and ecological benefits, urban agriculture reinforces the cultural identity of Cap-Haïtien and its agrarian traditions. It offers a locally grounded engine for recovery — one that generates livelihoods, strengthens community resilience and knits the city back together around a shared, productive landscape.
